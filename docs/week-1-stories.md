## mon 19 oct: the kuleshov effect
**fields:** cinema + psychology

**notification:** a film teacher made people cry using soup.

**cards**
1. [visual: blank face] this man is doing nothing! no smile. no frown.
2. [face + soup] a film teacher in russia put his face next to a bowl of soup. audiences said: he looks so hungry.
3. [face + coffin] same face. then a girl in a coffin. audiences said: so sad.
4. [face + woman on a divan] same face. then a woman on a divan. audiences said: he's in love!
5. [three faces, identical] it was the same shot every time! the filmmaker Vsevolod Pudovkin wrote in 1929 that audiences raved about the acting, even though the face never changed.
6. [white card: what it means] this is the kuleshov effect: your brain connects two things shown back to back, and makes up the link. alfred hitchcock showed it in a tv interview: his own squinting face, then a woman with a baby, and he looks like a kind old man. swap the baby for a woman in a bikini, and he looks like a dirty old man!
7. [why it is about you] you do this every day! the word "ok" feels different after a joke than after bad news. same word. different thing next to it.
8. [yellow card: say it out loud] put two things side by side and people write the story themselves!
9. [crew] when did someone misread your face? what was next to it?

**visual:** original illustration of the three-frame strip (our own, no licence issue). The actor was Ivan Mozzhukhin; no original footage is known to survive, so do not present any image as "the footage".

**takeaway line:** "put two things side by side and people write the story themselves."

**conversation starter:** when did someone read your silence the wrong way?

**rabbit holes:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuleshov_effect | https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/does-the-kuleshov-effect-really-exist-revisiting-a-classic-film-e-2/ (check length)

**quiz**
1. mcq: a director cuts from a man's blank face to a snowstorm. what will viewers most likely think he feels? (a) cold ✓ (b) angry (c) nothing. why: viewers invent a link between shots.
2. mcq: in the soup, coffin and divan version, what changed? (a) the actor's face (b) only what the face was cut next to ✓ (c) the lighting.
3. fill: "put two things ____ and people write the story themselves." (side by side)
4. best line: your friend says their boss replied with just "noted." which would you say at dinner?
   - a: "that's the kuleshov effect from some 1920s russian film." (name-drop, no use)
   - b ✓: "'noted' means nothing alone. after a joke it's friendly, after a deadline it's cold. same word, different thing next to it." (uses the idea on something real)
   - c: "people read too much into things." (vague)
5. optional, shared with crew: when did someone misread your face?

**caveats (show as fine print on the card):** the experiment's exact date and how it was made are not known; accounts differed from the start; no film or photo documentation is known. A 1992 replication (Prince and Hensley) did not find the effect; fMRI work in 2006 (Mobbs et al.) and a 2016 study (Barratt et al.) did.

**sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuleshov_effect

**still to confirm:** Pudovkin's 1929 text; Hitchcock's CBC Telescope interview (date, wording); hold the "dirty old man" line for tone review.

## thu 22 oct: the mozart effect
**fields:** psychology + music

**notification:** playing mozart to your baby won't make them smarter. the study never involved babies.

**cards**
1. [illustration: mozart + headphones] "mozart makes you smarter." you've heard it. here's what actually happened!
2. 1993: frances rauscher, gordon shaw and catherine ky published a short paper in the journal nature. 36 college students listened to a mozart sonata, a relaxation exercise, or silence.
3. then they did spatial puzzles from an iq test. after mozart, a small boost! it faded within about 15 minutes. and iq itself was never measured.
4. then the headlines happened! a 1994 new york times column said mozart "actually makes you smarter." a 1997 book stretched it to babies.
5. 1998: georgia's governor zell miller proposed $105,000 a year so every newborn in the state got a classical music cd. he reportedly played "ode to joy" for lawmakers and asked if they felt smarter!
6. later studies: mixed. a 2010 review of 39 studies found little evidence, with bigger effects in studies linked to the original researchers, and strong publication bias. other work suggests mood and arousal explain it.
7. [white: what it means] a small, specific result got repeated until it became a big, general claim. the study was real. the claim wasn't.
8. [yellow: say it out loud] a headline is a story about a study. it is not the study!
9. [crew] what "fact" did you believe that turned out to be a headline?

**visual:** a Mozart portrait. Pick a specific Wikimedia Commons file (for example, a posthumous portrait from c. 1780) and record its licence on the file page. Not yet picked.

**takeaway line:** "a headline is a story about a study, not the study."

## fri 23 oct: the trolley problem and 40 million decisions
**fields:** philosophy + psychology + technology

**notification:** 40 million decisions were collected on who a self-driving car should hit. the answers changed by country.

**cards**
1. [diagram] a runaway trolley will hit five people. you can pull a switch so it hits one person instead. what do you do?
2. most people say: pull the switch.
3. now: same five people, same trolley. but to stop it you must push a large man off a bridge. most people say: no!
4. same maths: five lives versus one. different answer! why?
5. the puzzle was introduced by the philosopher philippa foot in 1967. judith jarvis thomson named it the "trolley problem" in 1976.
6. 2018: mit's moral machine let people decide who a self-driving car should spare. about 40 million decisions, from 233 countries and territories. people broadly preferred sparing humans over pets, more people over fewer, and younger over older. but the strength of those preferences varied, in three broad clusters of countries.
7. [white: what it means] gut reactions aren't one rulebook. "right" can change with how the question is asked, and with where you grew up.
8. [yellow: say it out loud] same maths, different feeling. that's why ethics is hard!
9. [crew] would you push the man? what changed between the switch and the bridge?

**visual:** a trolley-problem diagram: use an illustration of our own, or a Wikimedia Commons diagram after checking its licence (not yet picked).

**takeaway line:** "same maths, different feeling. that's why ethics is hard."

**conversation starter:** would you pull the switch? would you push the man? what's different?
